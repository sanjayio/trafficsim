About TrafficSim

TrafficSim is a game about roads. You build a street network, put homes, shops and offices beside it, and watch the traffic those buildings make. When a junction jams, you see why, and you can try another layout.

What it's about

City builders usually treat traffic as one problem among many. Here it's the main thing. There are no budgets, taxes or disasters, only roads, junctions and the drivers on them. Build something pretty, or try to make a busy crossroads flow.

How the simulation works

Every car is simulated on its own, 60 times a second. A car leaves a home for a shop or office and plans the fastest route there, weighing speed limits and turns. Along the way it keeps a safe distance from the car ahead, picks its lanes several roads in advance, looks for gaps to change lane or join a roundabout, and obeys signals and signs. If it's held up too long, it looks for another way.

The rules drivers follow are settings, not fixed code: right of way, signal timings, the gaps they'll accept, how pushy they get near a turn. Each map keeps its own rules, so you can see what changes when drivers are patient or in a hurry.

How it's built

TrafficSim runs in the browser. The world is drawn in 3D with three.js, the menus and panels are React, and the simulation is plain TypeScript with its own tests. The same code can also be packaged as a desktop app with Tauri.

The code is on GitHub. See Contribute if you'd like to help.

Privacy

There are no accounts. Your maps stay in your browser unless you save them as a file. The radio, when you turn it on, streams from Radio Paradise.
